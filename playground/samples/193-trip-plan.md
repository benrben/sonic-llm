Trip plan: a week in Marlowcross

Planned by Tilda Novak, with Rosa Wexler joining for the whole trip. This page collects everything we need so that nobody has to scroll through old messages.

Dates and travel
We leave on January 4, 2027 and come back on January 14, 2027. The outbound flight is FL-9252, a morning departure from the main airport; check-in closes ninety minutes before take-off. Each of us may bring one suitcase and one small bag, and the airline allows 17 kg for the suitcase. From Dunmorewick we take the regional train, booking reference TR-7584, which runs every hour. The journey takes a little under two hours, so we should reach Marlowcross in the early evening.

Where we stay
We have booked Hotel Cormorant, a small family-run place near the old harbour. Breakfast is included. The room is on the second floor and has a view of the market square. The owners asked us to let them know if we will arrive after ten at night, and we have promised to send a message from the train. A deposit of 231 euros has been paid by Tilda Novak; the rest is due when we check out.

Money
The whole trip is expected to cost 1277 euros for the two of us, including flights, the hotel and a little spare money. A taxi from the station to the hotel costs about 41 euros if the buses have stopped. The museum we both want to see charges 22 euros per person on weekdays. We agreed to keep a shared list of what each person pays and to settle up after we are home.

Things to do
- Walk along the harbour wall on the first evening and eat something simple.
- Visit the museum on the second morning, before it gets crowded.
- Take the small ferry across the bay and have lunch on the other side.
- Leave one day completely free for whatever turns out to be nice.

Packing. We will take light clothes that can be layered, a rain jacket, comfortable walking shoes and one nicer outfit for the evening. The weather forecast promises around 24 degrees in the afternoon and cooler nights, with a chance of rain on two days. Sun cream, a small first aid kit, a reusable water bottle and an adapter for the sockets also go in the bag.

Getting around. The old town is small and easy to walk. A day ticket for local buses costs 26 euros, and the ticket machine at the station takes cards. Bicycles can be rented near the harbour, but the hills are steeper than they look. Bastian Quinlan, who visited last year, warns that the cobbled streets are hard on suitcase wheels.

Emergencies. The local number for the ambulance is 280, and the nearest pharmacy is two streets from the hotel. We have both taken out travel insurance, and a copy of the policy is saved in the shared folder. The bank card helpline is also written there. If one of us gets lost, we will meet at the fountain on the market square.

Wish list. If there is time we would like to see the lighthouse, walk the hill path to the ruined castle, and find a shop that sells local ceramics. Bastian Quinlan recommended the sunset from the old pier, and a small gallery that is open until 11 in the evening. We will decide day by day and not plan every hour.

Before we go
Check that passports are valid, download the offline map, tell the bank we will be abroad, and put a spare charger in the hand luggage. Rosa Wexler will bring the guidebook and Tilda Novak will print the booking pages.
