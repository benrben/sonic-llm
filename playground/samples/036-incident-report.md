Erratum

Erratum to the table: the unit price of RT-6095 is 319 euros, not 351 euros.

Costs. The incident used about 16 hours of engineering time in total, spread over several people. We offered no refunds, since nobody was charged for the time of the outage. The cost of the extra servers we started during the recovery was small, and they were shut down again the following day. The review meeting will take one hour.

Earlier incidents. A similar problem occurred some months ago and was recorded as ticket RF-968. At that time the cause was different, but the symptoms were the same, and the runbook did not tell the engineer where to look. We should have connected the two events earlier, and the review will look at how to make older reports easier to find.

Communication. The status page was updated three times, and each update was reviewed by Tomas Nakagawa before it was published. An internal message went to the wider team, and a short note was sent to customers who had contacted support. We believe the updates were honest and clear, although one of them used a technical term that customers did not know.

Lessons. Small changes can cause large problems when the checks are skipped. People who are tired make fewer good decisions, so the on-call rota will be changed so that nobody is the first responder for more than 16 nights in a row. Alerts should say what to do, and not only what is wrong, and every important service needs a named owner.

Metrics. The service normally answers in about 16 milliseconds and fails fewer than 11 requests in a thousand. During the incident the failure rate rose sharply and the average answer time went up more than tenfold. Within 31 minutes of the fix being applied, both numbers returned to normal values. Graphs of the period are attached to the ticket.
