Trip plan: a week in Ashbywick

Planned by Anouk Oyelaran, with Esme Okonkwo joining for the whole trip. This page collects everything we need so that nobody has to scroll through old messages.

Dates and travel
We leave on 17 March 2027 and come back on 19 March 2027. The outbound flight is FL-7829, a morning departure from the main airport; check-in closes ninety minutes before take-off. Each of us may bring one suitcase and one small bag, and the airline allows 19 kg for the suitcase. From Fenwickhaven we take the regional train, booking reference TR-2344, which runs every hour. The journey takes a little under two hours, so we should reach Ashbywick in the early evening.

Where we stay
We have booked Hotel Delphine, a small family-run place near the old harbour. Breakfast is included. The room is on the second floor and has a view of the market square. The owners asked us to let them know if we will arrive after ten at night, and we have promised to send a message from the train. A deposit of 102 euros has been paid by Anouk Oyelaran; the rest is due when we check out.

Money
The whole trip is expected to cost 1895 euros for the two of us, including flights, the hotel and a little spare money. A taxi from the station to the hotel costs about 57 euros if the buses have stopped. The museum we both want to see charges 29 euros per person on weekdays. We agreed to keep a shared list of what each person pays and to settle up after we are home.

Things to do
- Walk along the harbour wall on the first evening and eat something simple.
- Visit the museum on the second morning, before it gets crowded.
- Take the small ferry across the bay and have lunch on the other side.
- Leave one day completely free for whatever turns out to be nice.

Getting around. The old town is small and easy to walk. A day ticket for local buses costs 54 euros, and the ticket machine at the station takes cards. Bicycles can be rented near the harbour, but the hills are steeper than they look. Celia Okonkwo, who visited last year, warns that the cobbled streets are hard on suitcase wheels.

Wish list. If there is time we would like to see the lighthouse, walk the hill path to the ruined castle, and find a shop that sells local ceramics. Celia Okonkwo recommended the sunset from the old pier, and a small gallery that is open until 8 in the evening. We will decide day by day and not plan every hour.

Eating out. A colleague recommended a small restaurant called Copperleaf Studio near the market, which has a short menu and fair prices. We expect to spend about 54 euros per person on a good dinner and much less on lunch. Cafes in Marlowcross often close early on Sundays, so we will buy bread and fruit the day before if we want a picnic.

Packing. We will take light clothes that can be layered, a rain jacket, comfortable walking shoes and one nicer outfit for the evening. The weather forecast promises around 27 degrees in the afternoon and cooler nights, with a chance of rain on two days. Sun cream, a small first aid kit, a reusable water bottle and an adapter for the sockets also go in the bag.

Before we go
Check that passports are valid, download the offline map, tell the bank we will be abroad, and put a spare charger in the hand luggage. Esme Okonkwo will bring the guidebook and Anouk Oyelaran will print the booking pages.
