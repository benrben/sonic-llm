Trip plan: a week in Sableford

Planned by Jonas Salazar, with Viktor Castellan joining for the whole trip. This page collects everything we need so that nobody has to scroll through old messages.

Dates and travel
We leave on 10 January 2027 and come back on 20 January 2027. The outbound flight is FL-6054, a morning departure from the main airport; check-in closes ninety minutes before take-off. Each of us may bring one suitcase and one small bag, and the airline allows 25 kg for the suitcase. From Norwickport we take the regional train, booking reference TR-6520, which runs every hour. The journey takes a little under two hours, so we should reach Sableford in the early evening.

Where we stay
We have booked Hotel Aurelia, a small family-run place near the old harbour. Breakfast is included. The room is on the second floor and has a view of the market square. The owners asked us to let them know if we will arrive after ten at night, and we have promised to send a message from the train. A deposit of 177 euros has been paid by Jonas Salazar; the rest is due when we check out.

Money
The whole trip is expected to cost 2184 euros for the two of us, including flights, the hotel and a little spare money. A taxi from the station to the hotel costs about 41 euros if the buses have stopped. The museum we both want to see charges 9 euros per person on weekdays. We agreed to keep a shared list of what each person pays and to settle up after we are home.

Things to do
- Walk along the harbour wall on the first evening and eat something simple.
- Visit the museum on the second morning, before it gets crowded.
- Take the small ferry across the bay and have lunch on the other side.
- Leave one day completely free for whatever turns out to be nice.

Getting around. The old town is small and easy to walk. A day ticket for local buses costs 31 euros, and the ticket machine at the station takes cards. Bicycles can be rented near the harbour, but the hills are steeper than they look. Sven Moreau, who visited last year, warns that the cobbled streets are hard on suitcase wheels.

Eating out. A colleague recommended a small restaurant called Bluepine Tools near the market, which has a short menu and fair prices. We expect to spend about 31 euros per person on a good dinner and much less on lunch. Cafes in Hollisstead often close early on Sundays, so we will buy bread and fruit the day before if we want a picnic.

Wish list. If there is time we would like to see the lighthouse, walk the hill path to the ruined castle, and find a shop that sells local ceramics. Sven Moreau recommended the sunset from the old pier, and a small gallery that is open until 10 in the evening. We will decide day by day and not plan every hour.

Emergencies. The local number for the ambulance is 895, and the nearest pharmacy is two streets from the hotel. We have both taken out travel insurance, and a copy of the policy is saved in the shared folder. The bank card helpline is also written there. If one of us gets lost, we will meet at the fountain on the market square.

Before we go
Check that passports are valid, download the offline map, tell the bank we will be abroad, and put a spare charger in the hand luggage. Viktor Castellan will bring the guidebook and Jonas Salazar will print the booking pages.
