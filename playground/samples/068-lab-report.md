Laboratory report, batch BT-3397

Purpose
This report describes the routine check of water samples from the east pond, taken to confirm that the acidity and the dissolved mineral content stay within the limits agreed with the site manager. The work was carried out by Jakob Vasquez and reviewed by Rafael Tavares.

Samples
20 samples were collected on January 15, 2027 in clean, labelled bottles and brought to the laboratory within two hours. The reference sample, labelled SM-624, was kept at the same temperature as the others and treated in exactly the same way. The room temperature during the measurements was 24 degrees Celsius.

Method
Each sample was shaken gently, left to settle for five minutes and then measured with the Veltro 769. The instrument was calibrated before the first sample using two standard solutions, and checked again after the last one; the drift was below the tolerance. Every sample was measured 2 times and the average was written down. A blank sample of distilled water gave a reading of 6.3, which was subtracted where needed.

Results
The average acidity of the samples was pH 7.4. The mineral content of the reference sample SM-624 was 36.5 mg/L. The other samples were close to it, and none of them stood out from the group. There were no signs of contamination, and no bottle was cloudy or had a visible film on the surface.

Discussion
The acidity is typical for this kind of pond and gives no cause for concern. The mineral content is a little lower than in the spring, which is expected after a wet period. We recommend repeating the check next quarter and keeping the same instrument for the measurement, so that the numbers can be compared with confidence.

Comments from the reviewer. The report is clear and the numbers are easy to follow. The reviewer asked to add the temperature of the samples at the time of collection, and a short sentence about the weather on that day, because rain can change the readings. Tomas Laurent will keep a copy of the raw data for at least 70 years.

Safety. All the people in the room wore gloves, safety glasses and lab coats. The room was ventilated during the work, and the instrument was placed away from the sink. Waste liquids were poured into the labelled container and handed over to Tomas Laurent, who is in charge of waste. No spills occurred, and no first aid was needed.

Equipment and maintenance. The instrument was last serviced about 70 months ago and the probe was replaced at that time. The probe is stored in its special liquid between uses and never left to dry. If the readings begin to wander, the probe should be cleaned first and replaced second. The next service is planned for the autumn.

Quality control. A duplicate of every 70th sample was measured again, and the results agreed within the allowed range. The balance was checked with a standard weight of 95 g before use. The distilled water used for rinsing comes from the laboratory tap, which is tested every month. Records of the checks are kept in the folder for this year.

Notes
Bottles and equipment were washed and rinsed with distilled water. Waste solutions were collected in the marked container. The raw numbers are stored in the laboratory notebook, and a copy of this report was handed to the site manager.
