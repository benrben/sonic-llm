# Keeping houseplants alive: a beginner's care guide

Most houseplants die from too much attention, not too little. Overwatering kills more plants than forgetting them. This guide
covers six common plants and the few habits that matter most.

## The three questions

Before buying a plant, answer these honestly:

1. **How much light does the spot get?** A south-facing window in the northern hemisphere is bright, with several hours of direct
   sun. An east window gives gentle morning sun. A north window or a corner more than two metres from the glass is low light.
2. **How often are you home?** If you travel often, choose plants that store water, like succulents and snake plants.
3. **Do you have pets?** Some plants, such as lilies, pothos and philodendrons, are toxic to cats and dogs.

## Six reliable plants

**Snake plant (Sansevieria).** Tolerates low light and neglect. Water every 3 to 4 weeks in summer and every 6 to 8 weeks in
winter. Root rot from wet soil is the only real danger. Mildly toxic to pets.

**Pothos.** A trailing vine that grows in bright to low light. Water when the top 3 cm of soil is dry, roughly once a week. The
leaves droop clearly when it is thirsty and recover within hours of watering. Toxic to pets.

**Spider plant.** Grows in bright indirect light and produces baby plantlets that you can pot up. Water weekly and use filtered or
rain water if the leaf tips go brown, which is a sign of too much fluoride. Safe for pets.

**ZZ plant.** Stores water in thick underground rhizomes and survives a month without water. Water only when the soil is
completely dry, about every 2 to 3 weeks. Toxic to pets.

**Peace lily.** Likes medium light and humid air. It droops dramatically when it needs water, and it tells you clearly. Water once
the leaves start to sag, typically every 7 to 10 days. It flowers best near an east window. Toxic to pets.

**Aloe vera.** Needs the brightest spot you have, with a few hours of direct sun. Water deeply every 2 to 3 weeks, and let the soil
dry completely in between. The gel in the leaves soothes minor burns.

## How to water properly

- Check the soil with a finger: if the top 2 to 3 cm are dry, water. If you are unsure, wait a day.
- Water slowly until some water runs out of the drainage holes, and empty the saucer after 15 minutes. Roots standing in water
  rot.
- Use room-temperature water. Very cold water can shock the roots.
- Water less in winter, when growth slows, and more in summer.

## Feeding and repotting

Feed with a balanced liquid fertiliser at half the recommended strength once a month from March to September. Do not feed in
winter. Repot when roots grow out of the drainage holes, usually every 18 to 24 months, into a pot only 2 to 4 cm wider. A pot
that is too big holds too much wet soil. The best time is spring.

## Common problems

- **Yellow lower leaves:** usually too much water. Let the soil dry out and check the drainage.
- **Brown crispy tips:** low humidity or fluoride in the water. Group plants together or use filtered water.
- **Leggy, stretched growth:** not enough light. Move the plant closer to a window.
- **Tiny flying insects:** fungus gnats, attracted by wet soil. Let the top layer dry and add a layer of sand.
- **White cotton spots on stems:** mealybugs. Wipe with cotton dipped in rubbing alcohol and repeat weekly.
