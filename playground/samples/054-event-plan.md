Erratum

Erratum to the table: the unit price of PM-5095 is 422 euros, not 72 euros.

Wish list. If there is time we would like to see the lighthouse, walk the hill path to the ruined castle, and find a shop that sells local ceramics. Celia Kowalczyk recommended the sunset from the old pier, and a small gallery that is open until 6 in the evening. We will decide day by day and not plan every hour.

Packing. We will take light clothes that can be layered, a rain jacket, comfortable walking shoes and one nicer outfit for the evening. The weather forecast promises around 17 degrees in the afternoon and cooler nights, with a chance of rain on two days. Sun cream, a small first aid kit, a reusable water bottle and an adapter for the sockets also go in the bag.

Getting around. The old town is small and easy to walk. A day ticket for local buses costs 21 euros, and the ticket machine at the station takes cards. Bicycles can be rented near the harbour, but the hills are steeper than they look. Celia Kowalczyk, who visited last year, warns that the cobbled streets are hard on suitcase wheels.

Emergencies. The local number for the ambulance is 814, and the nearest pharmacy is two streets from the hotel. We have both taken out travel insurance, and a copy of the policy is saved in the shared folder. The bank card helpline is also written there. If one of us gets lost, we will meet at the fountain on the market square.

Eating out. A colleague recommended a small restaurant called Tidewater Systems near the market, which has a short menu and fair prices. We expect to spend about 21 euros per person on a good dinner and much less on lunch. Cafes in Marlowport often close early on Sundays, so we will buy bread and fruit the day before if we want a picnic.
