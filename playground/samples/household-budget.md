# Our household budget and savings plan for the year

Written by Maya and Daniel Okafor in January. We review it on the first Sunday of every month, over breakfast, and change
anything that has stopped matching reality.

## Where the money comes from

Our combined take-home pay is 4,350 euros a month: Maya 2,400 euros and Daniel 1,950 euros. Daniel also earns about 300 euros a
quarter from weekend tutoring, which we treat as a bonus and do not plan around. There is no other regular income.

## Fixed costs: 2,180 euros a month

| Item | Monthly cost |
|------|--------------|
| Rent | 1,350 |
| Electricity and heating | 140 |
| Internet and two phone plans | 75 |
| Public transport passes | 96 |
| Contents and liability insurance | 24 |
| Streaming and subscriptions | 29 |
| Loan repayment (Daniel's laptop) | 66 |
| Gym membership (Maya) | 40 |
| Childcare for Zoe, two afternoons | 360 |

The streaming line was 52 euros until March. We cancelled two services nobody was watching, which saves 23 euros a month, or
276 euros a year.

## Flexible costs: 1,300 euros a month

- Groceries: 620 euros. We shop once a week with a list and plan five dinners ahead; the other two are leftovers.
- Eating out and coffee: 110 euros, no more than two restaurant meals a month.
- Clothes and shoes: 100 euros, with most of it spent on Zoe, who outgrows shoes every four months.
- Health and pharmacy: 70 euros.
- Gifts and birthdays: 80 euros.
- Home and garden: 90 euros.
- Fun money: 100 euros each for Maya and Daniel, spent without explaining.
- Car sharing: 30 euros on average.

## Savings: the 50 percent rule of what is left

After fixed and flexible costs about 870 euros remain. We split it like this:

1. **Emergency fund: 300 euros a month** until it reaches six months of fixed costs, which is 13,080 euros. The balance on 1
   January was 4,900 euros, so the fund will be complete in about 27 months.
2. **Holiday fund: 150 euros a month**, for a two-week trip in August. The target is 1,800 euros.
3. **Pension top-up: 200 euros a month** into Maya's personal plan, on top of what her employer pays in.
4. **Zoe's account: 50 euros a month**, which her grandparents match.
5. **Buffer: 170 euros a month** that stays in the current account for surprises. If nothing goes wrong, we move it to the
   emergency fund at the end of each quarter.

## Rules we agreed on

- Any purchase over 150 euros is discussed first, and anything over 500 euros waits one week.
- We do not use credit cards for everyday spending. The one card we keep has a limit of 800 euros and is paid in full each
  month.
- When one of us gets a raise, half of it goes to savings before we adapt our spending.
- Windfalls, such as tax refunds, are split: half to the emergency fund, a quarter to the holiday fund and a quarter to a
  treat we choose together.

## What we learned last year

The biggest surprise was how much we spent on small, unplanned things: about 85 euros a month on snacks and delivery fees we had
never counted. Putting those into a named category, with a cap of 40 euros, cut the total almost in half within three months.
We also noticed that annual bills, for insurance and the bike service, made some months look bad, so we now put 20 euros a month
into an envelope for them.
