# Sourdough bread at home: a weekend recipe

This is the loaf the Alder Street baking club makes most often. It needs about 20 minutes of work spread over a day and a
half, and almost no equipment: a large bowl, a lidded cast-iron pot and an oven.

## Ingredients (one loaf, about 900 g)

- 450 g strong white bread flour
- 50 g whole wheat flour
- 375 g water at 28 degrees C (lukewarm, not hot)
- 100 g active sourdough starter (fed 6 to 8 hours earlier and bubbly)
- 10 g fine sea salt

The dough is 75 percent hydration: the water weighs three quarters as much as the flour. That makes it sticky at first, and
that is normal. Wet your hands instead of adding flour.

## Timeline

**Friday, 18:00 - Mix.** Stir the flours, water and starter until no dry flour is left. Cover the bowl and wait 45 minutes.
This rest is called the autolyse; it lets the flour soak up the water so the dough needs less kneading.

**Friday, 18:45 - Salt and fold.** Sprinkle the salt over the dough with a splash of water and pinch it in. Then do a set of
folds: lift one side of the dough, stretch it up and fold it over the middle. Turn the bowl and repeat four times.

**Friday, 19:15 to 21:45 - Bulk rise.** Do three more sets of folds, one every 30 minutes, then leave the dough alone. In a
kitchen at 22 degrees C the bulk rise takes about 5 hours in total. The dough is ready when it has grown by about half, the
top is domed and you can see bubbles at the edge of the bowl.

**Friday, 23:00 - Shape.** Tip the dough onto a lightly floured table, fold the corners to the centre, flip it over and drag it
towards you until the surface is tight. Place it seam side up in a floured basket or a bowl lined with a clean tea towel.

**Friday night to Saturday morning - Cold proof.** Cover the basket and put it in the fridge for 10 to 14 hours. The cold slows
the yeast and lets the flavour develop. A longer proof gives a more sour loaf.

**Saturday, 09:00 - Bake.** Put the empty pot with its lid in the oven and heat it to 245 degrees C for 45 minutes. Tip the cold
dough onto baking paper, score the top with a sharp knife about 1 cm deep, lower it into the hot pot with the paper, and put
the lid on. Bake 25 minutes with the lid on, then 20 minutes with the lid off at 220 degrees C.

**Saturday, 10:30 - Cool.** Let the loaf cool on a rack for at least one hour. Cutting too early makes the crumb gummy because
the steam is still setting the crumb.

## Troubleshooting

- **Flat loaf.** The starter was probably weak. Feed it twice before baking, and check that it doubles within 6 hours.
- **Dense, tight crumb.** The dough was under-proofed. Give it another hour of bulk rise next time.
- **Pale crust.** The pot was not hot enough. Preheat for the full 45 minutes and take off the lid for the last stage.
- **Too sour.** Shorten the cold proof to 8 hours, or use a younger starter.

## Keeping the starter alive

Keep about 50 g of starter in a jar in the fridge. Once a week, take it out, discard all but 20 g, and feed it with 40 g of
flour and 40 g of water. Leave it on the counter for two hours, then return it to the fridge. Before a bake, feed it at room
temperature and wait until it doubles. The club's oldest starter, called Marguerite, has lived in the same jar since 2016.
