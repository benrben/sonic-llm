Ending the lease. At the end, the tenant returns all keys, cleans the flat and sets up a time for the final inspection with the landlord. Any difference between the first photo list and the final state will be discussed in a friendly way. The landlord keeps a list of about 21 recommended cleaners, and the tenant may use any of them or do the work personally.

Insurance. The landlord insures the building and the shared parts. The tenant is advised to take out a contents insurance policy and to include cover for accidental damage and water. A copy of the policy should be handed to the landlord on request. A neighbour, Jonas Eriksen, has agreed to keep a spare key for emergencies, and the landlord knows about this arrangement.

Utilities and services. Water and the shared electricity in the hallway are included in the heating charge. The tenant arranges and pays for their own electricity and internet, and must give the meter readings on the day they move in; the starting reading on the electricity meter was 650. Rubbish collection is on 21 days of the month, and bins must be put out the evening before.

Changes and repairs. The tenant may paint the walls in light colours if the flat is returned in white at the end. Drilling holes for shelves is allowed, but large nails and sticky hooks on tiles are not. The flat has 21 windows, all of which were checked by the landlord before the tenant moved in. Broken glass from normal use is the tenant's cost.
