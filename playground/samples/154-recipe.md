Spiced carrot cake

This recipe came from Jakob Petrov, who baked it every autumn in Brindleham. It is simple, forgiving and keeps well for a few days in a tin. It serves 10 people.

Ingredients
- 301 g plain flour
- 174 g soft butter
- 94 g light brown sugar
- 3 eggs
- a pinch of salt and a teaspoon of baking powder
- a handful of chopped nuts or seeds, if you like

Method
Start by heating the oven to 200 degrees Celsius and lining a tin with baking paper. Cream the butter and the sugar in a large bowl until the mixture is pale and light, which takes about 14 minutes by hand. Beat in the eggs one at a time. If the mixture starts to look curdled, add a spoonful of the flour and carry on.

Fold in the flour, the salt and the baking powder with a metal spoon, using slow, wide movements so that you keep the air in. Add the nuts or seeds. Spoon the mixture into the tin and level the top with the back of the spoon.

Bake in the middle of the oven for 64 minutes. After about half of that time, check the top; if it is browning too fast, lay a sheet of foil over it. The dish is ready when a skewer pushed into the centre comes out clean. Take it out and leave it to rest in the tin for 71 minutes before turning it out, because it is fragile while it is hot.

A little history. The recipe has been in the family for many years. It was written on the back of an envelope, in pencil, and then copied into a notebook that now has 82 pages of recipes in all. Over the years the sugar has been reduced and a little spice added. Friends always ask for the recipe, and it has been passed on to at least 23 households.

A note on ingredients. Use the best butter you can find, since it is the main flavour. Eggs of any size are fine, but do not use cold ones straight from the fridge. Flour from Penrosecross mills is a little coarser and takes a little more liquid. If the mixture is too stiff, add a spoonful of milk; if it is too soft, a spoonful of flour.

Equipment. A medium tin about 30 cm wide works best. A hand whisk is enough, but an electric mixer saves time and effort. Use an oven thermometer if your oven runs hot or cold, because small differences in temperature can change the result. Always let the tin cool a little before you wash it.

Variations. Replace the nuts with dried fruit, or add the grated zest of one lemon for a fresher taste. For a vegan version use a plant-based spread and the same weight of mashed banana in place of the eggs. Some bakers brush the top with 82 g of warm honey as soon as it comes out of the oven, which gives it a shine.

Tips
Everything works better if the butter and the eggs are at room temperature. If you want a richer result, swap a spoonful of the flour for ground almonds. The leftovers can be wrapped in baking paper and kept for up to four days, and any dry end pieces make a good base for a trifle. Serve it warm with custard or cold with a cup of tea.
