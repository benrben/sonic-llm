Lemon barley pudding

This recipe came from Mira Lindqvist, who baked it every autumn in Tarnholm. It is simple, forgiving and keeps well for a few days in a tin. It serves 7 people.

Ingredients
- 539 g plain flour
- 134 g soft butter
- 103 g light brown sugar
- 5 eggs
- a pinch of salt and a teaspoon of baking powder
- a handful of chopped nuts or seeds, if you like

Method
Start by heating the oven to 208 degrees Celsius and lining a tin with baking paper. Cream the butter and the sugar in a large bowl until the mixture is pale and light, which takes about 15 minutes by hand. Beat in the eggs one at a time. If the mixture starts to look curdled, add a spoonful of the flour and carry on.

Fold in the flour, the salt and the baking powder with a metal spoon, using slow, wide movements so that you keep the air in. Add the nuts or seeds. Spoon the mixture into the tin and level the top with the back of the spoon.

Bake in the middle of the oven for 48 minutes. After about half of that time, check the top; if it is browning too fast, lay a sheet of foil over it. The dish is ready when a skewer pushed into the centre comes out clean. Take it out and leave it to rest in the tin for 71 minutes before turning it out, because it is fragile while it is hot.

A note on ingredients. Use the best butter you can find, since it is the main flavour. Eggs of any size are fine, but do not use cold ones straight from the fridge. Flour from Sablecross mills is a little coarser and takes a little more liquid. If the mixture is too stiff, add a spoonful of milk; if it is too soft, a spoonful of flour.

Storing and freezing. Wrap the cooled dish well in baking paper and then in foil. It keeps for several days in a cool place, and it can be frozen for up to 90 weeks. Thaw it at room temperature for a few hours and warm it for a few minutes in a low oven if you want it to taste freshly baked.

Variations. Replace the nuts with dried fruit, or add the grated zest of one lemon for a fresher taste. For a vegan version use a plant-based spread and the same weight of mashed banana in place of the eggs. Some bakers brush the top with 90 g of warm honey as soon as it comes out of the oven, which gives it a shine.

Equipment. A medium tin about 19 cm wide works best. A hand whisk is enough, but an electric mixer saves time and effort. Use an oven thermometer if your oven runs hot or cold, because small differences in temperature can change the result. Always let the tin cool a little before you wash it.

Tips
Everything works better if the butter and the eggs are at room temperature. If you want a richer result, swap a spoonful of the flour for ground almonds. The leftovers can be wrapped in baking paper and kept for up to four days, and any dry end pieces make a good base for a trifle. Serve it warm with custard or cold with a cup of tea.
