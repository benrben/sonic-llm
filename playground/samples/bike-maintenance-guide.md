# Keeping your commuter bike healthy: a monthly guide

A bike that is checked for ten minutes a month rarely leaves its owner stranded. This guide is written for a typical commuter
bike with rim or disc brakes and a chain drive, ridden about 60 km a week. If you ride more than that, shorten the intervals.

## Every ride: the ABC check (one minute)

- **A for air.** Press the tyres with your thumb. They should feel firm. Most commuter tyres want between 3.5 and 5 bar; the
  range is printed on the tyre wall. Low pressure causes most punctures.
- **B for brakes.** Squeeze each lever with the bike standing still. The lever should stop before it touches the handlebar.
- **C for chain.** The chain should look slightly oily, not dry and not black and gritty.

## Every month: the ten-minute service

1. **Wash the bike** with a bucket of warm water and a soft brush. Avoid pointing a jet washer at the bearings, because it
   pushes water in.
2. **Clean and lube the chain.** Wipe the chain with a rag while turning the pedals backwards. Add one drop of lubricant on
   each roller, turn the pedals for a minute, then wipe off the excess. Too much oil attracts dirt.
3. **Check the brake pads.** Rim brake pads have grooves; if the grooves are almost gone, replace the pads. Disc brake pads
   should be at least 1.5 mm thick; below that the metal backing will damage the disc.
4. **Check the tyres** for cuts, embedded glass and sidewall cracks. Pull out any glass with tweezers.
5. **Tighten the bolts** on the stem, handlebar, seat post and wheels. Use a torque wrench if you have one: stem bolts are
   usually 5 to 6 newton metres, and the wheel axle nuts 30 to 40.
6. **Test the lights.** Replace batteries or recharge, and make sure the bracket does not slip.

## Every six months

- Check the chain wear with a chain gauge. A chain that is 0.75 percent stretched should be replaced; waiting until 1 percent
  also wears out the cassette, which costs three times as much.
- Re-grease the seat post so it does not seize in the frame.
- Check the cables for fraying where they enter the housing.
- Have the wheels checked for true: spin each one and watch the gap at the brake pads.

## Fixing a puncture on the roadside

You need a spare inner tube, two tyre levers, a pump and a patch kit. Take the wheel off, let all the air out, work one lever
under the tyre bead and slide the second lever around the rim. Pull out the old tube, and feel inside the tyre for the sharp
object that caused the puncture. Put in a slightly inflated new tube, refit the tyre starting opposite the valve, and pump to
the right pressure. The whole job takes about 15 minutes after a little practice.

## When to go to a shop

If the brakes squeal after cleaning, the gears jump under load, there is a creak that does not go away after greasing, or the
wheel wobbles from side to side, it is time to let a mechanic look. A basic service at a local shop costs around 60 euros and
lasts a year for a commuter rider.
